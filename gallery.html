The Gallery

Some of the best legal informatics in the country was built by law librarians, and most of it lives behind a URL where nobody walks past it. This is the wall. Wander through the halls, hover over anything that catches your eye, and step closer to visit the real thing.

I

Open Law & Data

The law belongs to the people it governs. These works pry primary law and legal data out of PDFs and paywalls and hand it back as something you can search, count, and map.

  • Database · Open data

    More than six million U.S. court decisions, scanned from the Harvard Law School Library’s own bound reporters and released free. Researchers get bulk data, everyone else gets search.

    Step closer to Caselaw Access Project→

    Caselaw Access Project

    Harvard Library Innovation Lab, Harvard Law School Library

  • Database · Launching 2026

    Every Supreme Court Indian law case and how each justice voted. Professor Keith Richotte’s dataset, structured with a librarian’s eye and built into a public site.

    Step closer to Supreme Court Indian Law Database→

    Supreme Court Indian Law Database

    Keith Richotte, with Cas Laskowski (data engineering)

  • Database · Web map

    Light pollution laws, nationwide, in one searchable place for advocates and scholars. Built with the International Dark Sky Association, around real user research.

    Step closer to Light Pollution Law Database→The map

    Light Pollution Law Database

    International Dark Sky Association and Cas Laskowski

II

Preservation & Evidence

Libraries keep the record. These works keep citations from rotting and turn old paper records into evidence you can measure.

  • Tool · Link preservation

    Links in court opinions and law review footnotes rot. Perma.cc saves a snapshot of the page when you cite it and gives you a link that will not break.

    Step closer to Perma.cc→

    Perma.cc

    Harvard Library Innovation Lab, Harvard Law School Library

  • Story map · Geospatial

    Federal parcel records converted into mapped land designations, then added up: more than 750,000 acres granted to the University of Arizona. Two AALL awards in 2024.

    Step closer to UA Land Grant History→Project page

    UA Land Grant History

    Cas Laskowski, University of Arizona Law Library

III

AI & Practice

Law librarians were testing these tools before the hype cycle noticed them. This hall holds the work that teaches lawyers to use AI, check it, and build with it, and the profession-wide effort to keep librarians at the table.

  • Course site · AI literacy

    The companion site for Law Practice in the Age of AI & Big Data. One module for each role a lawyer plays with AI: user, evaluator, builder, steward.

    Step closer to The Workbench→

    The Workbench

    Cas Laskowski, University of Arizona

  • Report · Initiative

    Six regional roundtables, more than 100 participants, and a roadmap. Phase two is building a central organization, a knowledge hub, and tiered AI training for the profession.

    Step closer to the Future of Law Libraries Initiative→Final report (PDF)

    Future of Law Libraries Initiative

    Co-founded and led by Cas Laskowski, with partners at Suffolk, Georgia State, Stanford, Northwestern, and Ohio State

IV

Justice & the Profession

Informatics only matters if people can use it, and if the next generation of librarians can get in the door. These works go after both.

  • Scorecard · Empirical research

    All 50 states ranked on their medical debt consumer protections. Students built it, states changed statutes because of it, and it won the 2023 AALL Innovation in Technology Award.

    Step closer to the Medical Debt Policy Scorecard→

    Medical Debt Policy Scorecard

    Innovation for Justice, University of Arizona, with Cas Laskowski

  • Open textbook · Interactive modules

    Research taught as a sequence of questions rather than a list of databases. Free to read, with an interactive companion for practice.

    Step closer to Question Method of Legal Research→Interactive modules

    Question Method of Legal Research

    Cas Laskowski, University of Arizona Libraries

  • Program · Open handbooks

    A funded route into the profession: mentorship, an assistantship, and training on the unwritten rules. Three open handbooks so far, so anyone can start their own.

    Step closer to the Law Library Fellows Program→Start your own

    Law Library Fellows Program

    Directed by Cas Laskowski, University of Arizona Law Library
